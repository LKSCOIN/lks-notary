What gets recorded

The proof of the event, not the event's data

The fields above are canonicalised (JCS, RFC 8785), hashed, and that hash is written to the blockchain inside the same batch as every other proof — so a lot event inherits the block confirmation and the Bitcoin anchoring without costing a separate transaction.

Each event also commits to the one before it on the same lot. The blockchain proof shows when an event was recorded; the chain of hashes shows that the history was not reordered or quietly pruned afterwards.

What stays out: everything you did not type here. Recipes, measurements, supplier prices and personal data belong in your systems. Commit to them with a hash and you can still prove, years later, that the document you hold is the one that existed then.
